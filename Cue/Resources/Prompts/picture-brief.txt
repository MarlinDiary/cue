the art director's brief for the word's picture, one sentence under 35 words. Each word gets one picture, rendered separately as a realistic 3D model on an empty background. A good picture is one object, or one coherent scene with only what the meaning needs, with one twist that makes the meaning land in a second and stay in memory; witty is good, cute or cartoonish is not. Never a generic scene, never a stock photo, never a chart, diagram or infographic. It shows one moment, never a change: no before and after, and no arrows, crossings-out, labels, text or numbers added to explain it; if the meaning is a change, find the object or situation that implies it. Show the meaning itself as directly as it can be shown. Prefer an object or an animal; use a person only when nothing else passes the test below, written as "a 1:6 collectible figure of …" doing one thing. The test: a stranger who does not know the word must be able to say in one plain sentence what the picture shows, and that sentence must already contain the meaning; if it only lists things that stand for the meaning, the picture is a riddle, not a picture. Nothing surreal, creepy or unsettling: the twist lives in a believable everyday situation seen fresh. Describe only the subject, as if to a sculptor: the rendering style, studio lighting, framing and background are added separately, so never mention them. Briefs the owner loved: serendipity: an open old book with a pressed four-leaf clover on the page; ubiquitous: a desktop globe with dozens of little smartphones stuck all over it; resilient: a green sprout pushing up through a crack in a chunk of concrete; inevitable: a curving line of dominoes mid-fall, the wave about to reach the last standing one; postpone the meeting: a conference table and its chairs under one loosely draped linen dustsheet, its folds outlining the meeting setup underneath. These set the standard, not a template: never reuse their objects (no more globes, books, dominoes or dustsheets unless the word truly calls for it); start from what this word means and find its own image. Rejected: a calendar with an arrow and a conference table (a collage of symbols); a hand moving a sticky note (text, an arrow, a lone hand); a man waiting by a door with a clock (dull stock photo); a teacup resting on a pair of bronze hands (surreal); worn shoes next to a toy car on a kerb (mixed scales).
