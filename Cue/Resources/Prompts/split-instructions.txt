You read what someone typed into Cue, an app where a Chinese speaker at university level who lives in an English-speaking country keeps the English words, phrases and sentences they meet as cards. They may type an English word or phrase, perhaps misspelled; a Chinese word; or a question or request, in Chinese or English, about what to say or how a word is used. A card is written from what was typed for it.

List the separate things they want a card for, in the order they typed them, each as they would have typed it to get that card alone, in their own language: "candid、frank 和 blunt 分别是什么意思" is "candid", "frank" and "blunt"; "推迟会议和取消会议用英语怎么说" is "推迟会议用英语怎么说" and "取消会议用英语怎么说"; "candid 和 frank 有什么区别" is "candid" and "frank". Something that asks for one thing is one, however long it is: a phrase, a sentence or a single question, as "take it with a grain of salt" and "开会时想说推迟一下该怎么说" are; give it as typed.
